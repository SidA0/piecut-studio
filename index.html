Each point is a STEP cut plan. Lower on the chart means less centerline error; further right means more estimated outside weld length. Select a point to inspect it, then generate its STEP package.

Path length Δ is the total centerline length of the proposed straight cylindrical pie pieces minus the centerline length of the original bent tube from the uploaded STEP. It is summed across the selected tube bodies, including their straight runs; it does not compare two adjacent pie pieces. A negative value means the pie-cut path is shorter. RMS and maximum error compare those same proposed piece centerlines with the original tube centerlines. Exact occupied-volume error is calculated after generating a STEP assembly.

01

Start the local CAD companion

Download the companion ZIP, extract it, and run Start_PieCut_Website.bat. Keep the console window open. The status at the top of this page changes to Local CAD Engine Ready.

The hosted page is the interface; OpenCascade runs at 127.0.0.1:8765 on your computer. If direct connection is blocked, click Open local workspace.

02

Upload the source STEP and select bodies

Return to Bent tube → pie cuts and choose a .step or .stp file. For a multi-body upload, check only the tube bodies you want converted. A supported body must be an open-ended, constant-wall round tube built from straight lines and circular arcs.

The example uses three outside diameters—31.75, 38.10, and 50.80 mm—with a 1.00 mm wall. Unsupported solids stay disabled and include a reason.

03

Choose the cut resolution

Maximum turn per pie cut controls the automatic starting plan. Smaller turns create more pieces and welds, but normally follow the source centerline more closely. You can then change the segment count for each detected bend.

  • Enable Resolution trade study to graph weld length against centerline RMS error.
  • Use Find shared cut plans to trade a few extra parts for fewer custom profiles.
  • Standard middle cuts by radius is experimental. It reuses middle sectors and leaves custom end or transition pieces to hit the exact terminal planes.
04

Inspect the overlay and accuracy metrics

Rotate the live preview and confirm the orange pie-cut plan follows the source tube. Pay attention to centerline RMS and maximum error, occupied-volume error, and path length Δ. Path length Δ is the proposed straight-piece centerline length minus the original bent centerline length across the selected bodies.

In this example, the centerline RMS error is 0.882 mm, the maximum is 1.851 mm, and the path is 9.248 mm shorter than the source. Use these values to compare plans; inspect the generated STEP for the final geometry decision.

05

Generate and review the ZIP

Click Generate cut package. The progress bar covers piece creation, volume comparison, assembly export, optional jig construction, and packaging. Start your review with these files:

all_piecuts_assembly.step
Every selected body and separate piece in its original position.
global_cut_profiles.csv
Quantity schedule for shared profiles.
unique_profiles/G###.step
One representative solid for each laser-cut profile.
fabrication_report.json
Counts, weld length, accuracy, and assumptions.
06

Order, label, fit, and weld

Send the profile STEP files and the quantity schedule to the tube laser company. Ask the shop to preserve the profile IDs. The example produced 46 pie pieces, 15 straight pieces, 54 matched joints, and 5,785.23 mm of calculated outside miter seam.

The weld number is seam circumference, not deposited bead length. It excludes bead width, root gap, overlap, shrinkage, and additional passes. Dry-fit the complete assembly and test the printed jig away from welding heat before production use.
